The problem

How does an AI company look as credible as the person who started it?

Most of the trust sat with the founder. Delivery, the depth of the team and the ways to engage were barely communicated, so enterprise buyers had to take the work on faith. They wanted evidence: who builds it, how it is run and where it has worked before.

What buyers asked
  • Who is the team?
  • How do you deliver?
  • Where has it worked?
  • How do we start?

My role

Designer, from workshop to handoff

I led the benchmarking, the stakeholder workshops and the insight synthesis, then the brand strategy and positioning, the identity refresh, the information architecture and the desktop and mobile UI. I worked with one junior designer, and with the client’s developers on the build.

Decision 1

Workshops before any design.

Before drawing a screen I ran two days of workshops with the leadership team. Day one covered growth focus, the gap between how the brand is seen and how it should be seen, personality, audiences and a future headline. Day two covered positioning, references, the trust and conversion journey and a longer-term roadmap. I synthesised it into a twelve-section insights deck that every later decision points back to.

Two days, one board

Decision 2

Sit between strategy and execution.

Benchmarking across four groups of competitors showed a gap. Strategic consultancies advise but rarely ship; low-cost vendors ship but rarely shape the problem. The client does both, so the positioning became: turn complex AI ideas into production-ready products. Four pillars back it up: deep AI expertise, end-to-end ownership, delivery excellence and strong engineering talent.

Finding the open space

Decision 3

Structure the site for buyers, not browsers.

The old site read like a brochure: visitors could browse it, but nothing moved them towards a conversation. I rebuilt the information architecture around how an enterprise buyer actually decides: establish relevance, evaluate fit, build confidence, then start the conversation. Each step has its own pages and its own way to convert, from gated reports early in research to one Book a Consultation everywhere, and every key journey stays within three clicks of it.

From visitor to buyer

Decision 4

Refine the logo, don’t replace it.

The existing mark already had recognition, so I refined it rather than starting over, and built the system around it: a navy and energetic blue palette with orange as a warm accent, Epilogue for headings and Manrope for body copy. The moodboard direction I recommended was strategic, energetic and precise. The guidelines cover how all of it is used.
